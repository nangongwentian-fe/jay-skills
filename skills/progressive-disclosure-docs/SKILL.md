# Progressive Disclosure Docs

Put information where a future reader would look for it. A small edit to a focused document does not need restructuring or an additional workflow.

## Choose the structure

- Inspect the target document and relevant neighboring topics; do not map the entire repository before every edit.
- Keep a short document intact when it serves one reader intent. Split when content has a different owner, audience or task, or when uncommon details obscure the common path.
- Use the entrypoint for purpose, essential constraints and conditional links. Put substantial procedures, schemas, examples or troubleshooting in the relevant topic document.
- Explain when a linked reference is useful. Do not require readers to preload every reference, or copy the same instructions into multiple layers.
- Preserve the target language and project naming conventions. Otherwise use descriptive lowercase English filenames with hyphens and a title appropriate to the audience.

## Keep authority clear

Current product behavior belongs with its implementation and product docs; decision records preserve reasons and tradeoffs. Follow the project's existing decision-record lifecycle, bilingual requirements, archive protections and validation rules. Organizational cleanup does not authorize editing frozen history or weakening required governance.

Keep personal preferences and environment-specific access details in their established authority. Shared templates should link to the active mechanism instead of embedding a private policy copy.

## Verify and finish

Check that links resolve, moved material remains discoverable and unique constraints survive. Use focused Markdown or repository checks where available. Synchronize actual behavior changes with their owning docs, then report the relevant changes and limitations briefly.
